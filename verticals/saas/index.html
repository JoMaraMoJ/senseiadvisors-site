The problem

Where SaaS accounting breaks.

Subscription revenue looks simple from the outside. Inside the ledger, it breaks in predictable places — and each one surfaces at the worst time: a board meeting, a fundraise, a diligence request.

Deferred revenue that doesn't tie

Balances live in spreadsheets, and revenue follows billing instead of performance. The waterfall and the contract file tell two different stories.

MRR that moves unexplained

New, expansion, contraction, and churn blend into one number that changes every time someone rebuilds it. Nobody can say why it moved.

Side letters outside the system

Terms agreed in email never reach the contract file — and quietly change what the revenue should be. A control gap and a fraud risk in one.

Billing that ≠ the books

Subscriptions, proration, failed payments, and dunning run in the billing system. The ledger gets a summary — and the differences accumulate.

Costs handled inconsistently

Sales commissions and internal-use software are capitalized, expensed, or forgotten depending on the month — ASC 340-40 and ASC 350-40 applied by mood.

Reporting rebuilt by hand

Every month, someone assembles the investor pack from the general ledger again. Runway and KPIs are a project, not an output.

The system

Built once, for SaaS. Run every month.

Sensei FinanceOS for SaaS is a complete close system — revenue, billing, costs, and reporting — installed on your existing accounting system, whatever that system is.

01

Revenue recognition — ASC 606

Performance obligations identified per contract, recognition scheduled against delivery, and side letters captured as a control — so recognized revenue is revenue you can defend.

02

Deferred revenue waterfall

The waterfall rolls by customer and contract — billings, recognition, remaining balance — and completes at Gate G1, Workday +1.

03

MRR / ARR bridge

New, expansion, contraction, and churn bridged every month to $0 unexplained at Gate G2 — with net revenue retention computed from the same numbers, not a second set.

04

Subscription billing, reconciled

Billing activity — subscriptions, proration, dunning, collections — reconciled to the ledger monthly, so the billing system and the books cannot drift apart.

05

Contract costs & capitalized software

Commissions under ASC 340-40 and internal-use software under ASC 350-40, capitalized and amortized on a stated policy — the same way every month.

06

Investor reporting

Cash, runway, and the KPI pack produced from closed books at Gate G3 — decision-ready and investor-ready, because the statements already tie.

Audit-ready

Designed to be audited: the PCAOB layer.

Every Sensei FinanceOS build is built to US GAAP, with SEC-reporting discipline, SOX-ready controls, and a PCAOB layer — audit evidence and workpaper discipline built to PCAOB standards.

After the close gates pass, a separate Gate G4 — Audit-Ready — checks evidence, workpapers, and review. A failed G4 blocks the words “audit-ready,” not the statements.

International

Built for US and international diligence: the International layer.

FinanceOS is one build with modular activation. Each client picks a US track or an International track — the system underneath is the same. Every standard is anchored two ways: to the FASB Codification and to IFRS Accounting Standards as issued by the IASB. FASB- and IASB-friendly by design, so a diligence team reads the same file on either side of the Atlantic — no translation.

Framework set per entity

Each entity carries its statutory framework, the group reporting framework, and its presentation currency on one register. Nothing is forced into a single framework.

The bridge close

Local statutory close first. Then the framework bridge. Then group consolidation, then the investor pack. The bridge ties with $0 unexplained — or the close does not pass Gate G5.

Instruments read both ways

SAFEs, convertibles, preferred shares, and warrants are analysed under both frameworks before signing — because the same instrument can be equity in one framework and a liability in the other.

Local rules stay local

Statutory calendars, indirect taxes, withholding, and R&D credit evidence are captured in registers, and local advisors file. FinanceOS never replaces the local statutory accountant.
