The problem

Where event accounting breaks.

A season looks simple from the stands. Inside the ledger, it breaks in predictable places — and each one surfaces at the worst time: a settlement dispute, a partner distribution, a diligence request.

Settlements that don't reach the books

Settlement sheets and the GL drift apart, and event revenue gets recognized from memory instead of the settlement.

Event profit known at season's end

Shared costs are allocated by habit, and nobody knows which events made money until it's too late to act.

Cash controls at the door

Box office and event-day cash move at volume, with controls that don't match the exposure.

Cancellations handled ad hoc

Postponed and cancelled events leave deferred revenue, refunds, and sunk costs scattered across the books.

Premium seating split by hand

Suites and club revenue are split outside the system, inconsistently, event after event.

A season of one-offs

Every event is treated as an exception. No cadence, no comparable history, no season view.

The system

Built once, for live events. Run every month.

Sensei FinanceOS for Sports & Entertainment is a complete close system — settlements, per-event P&L, cash controls, and season reporting — installed on your existing accounting system, whatever that system is.

01

Event settlements

Settlements tied to the GL at Gate G1, Workday +2 — posted from the settlement sheets, not reconstructed after the fact.

02

Per-event P&L

Every event bridged to $0 unexplained at Gate G2: revenue, direct costs, allocated shared costs, and margin.

03

Cash controls

Event-day cash, box office, and counts handled under stated controls that match the volume.

04

Cancellations & deferrals

A playbook for postponed and cancelled events: deferred revenue, refunds, and sunk costs, each in its place.

05

Premium & hospitality

Suites and club splits handled inside the system, the same way at every event.

06

Season reporting

Three-statement ties at Gate G3, and a season view event by event — margins comparable across the season.

Audit-ready

Designed to be audited: the PCAOB layer.

Every Sensei FinanceOS build is built to US GAAP, with SEC-reporting discipline, SOX-ready controls, and a PCAOB layer — audit evidence and workpaper discipline built to PCAOB standards.

After the close gates pass, a separate Gate G4 — Audit-Ready — checks evidence, workpapers, and review. A failed G4 blocks the words “audit-ready,” not the statements.

International

Built for US and international diligence: the International layer.

FinanceOS is one build with modular activation. Each client picks a US track or an International track — the system underneath is the same. Every standard is anchored two ways: to the FASB Codification and to IFRS Accounting Standards as issued by the IASB. FASB- and IASB-friendly by design, so a diligence team reads the same file on either side of the Atlantic — no translation.

Framework set per entity

Each entity carries its statutory framework, the group reporting framework, and its presentation currency on one register. Nothing is forced into a single framework.

The bridge close

Local statutory close first. Then the framework bridge. Then group consolidation, then the investor pack. The bridge ties with $0 unexplained — or the close does not pass Gate G5.

Instruments read both ways

SAFEs, convertibles, preferred shares, and warrants are analysed under both frameworks before signing — because the same instrument can be equity in one framework and a liability in the other.

Local rules stay local

Statutory calendars, indirect taxes, withholding, and R&D credit evidence are captured in registers, and local advisors file. FinanceOS never replaces the local statutory accountant.
